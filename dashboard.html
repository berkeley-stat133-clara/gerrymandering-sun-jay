This analysis uses official election data from the California Secretary of State and the Statewide Database:

  • 2024 General Election Results: Precinct-level statement of vote (SV and SR precincts)
  • AB 604 District Boundaries: Census block assignments from the Assembly Elections Committee
  • Candidate Information: Party affiliations for all congressional candidates
  • Census Block Data: Registration precinct to block correspondence from Statewide Database

All data accessed October-November 2025.

Part 2: Cleaning 2024 Election Data
  1. Loaded 51,123 precinct-level records from 2024 General Election
  2. Handled suppressed values (marked as “***” for privacy) by converting to NA
  3. Converted vote columns to numeric type
  4. Validated precinct key uniqueness and district assignments
  5. Aggregated votes to 53 congressional districts
Part 5: Simulating AB 604 Results (Proportional Allocation)
  1. Loaded SR precinct voting data (geographic precincts with stable boundaries)
  2. Obtained census block-to-precinct mapping with voter registration counts
  3. Loaded AB 604 block assignments (519,723 census blocks mapped to 52 new districts)
  4. Allocated SR precinct votes to census blocks proportionally based on registered voters
  5. Aggregated census block votes to AB 604 districts

This approach assumes voters are distributed proportionally to registered voters within each precinct.

Mean-Median Score

Measures asymmetry in vote distribution. Calculated as the difference between mean and median vote share across districts for one party.

  • Positive score: The favored party’s votes are “packed” into fewer districts
  • Negative score: The opposing party’s votes are packed
  • Near zero: More symmetric distribution
Efficiency Gap

Measures wasted votes - votes that don’t contribute to winning a seat. Wasted votes include:

  • All votes for losing candidates
  • Surplus votes (above 50%) for winning candidates

Formula: (Wasted votes for party A - Wasted votes for party B) / Total votes

  • Positive gap: Party A wastes more votes (disadvantaged)
  • Negative gap: Party B wastes more votes (disadvantaged)
  • Near zero: Votes wasted equally

  1. Single Election Analysis: These metrics use only 2024 election results. Gerrymandering assessment is more robust across multiple election cycles.

  2. Third-Party Candidates: Our analysis focuses on Democrat vs Republican races. Districts with significant third-party or independent candidates may have different dynamics.

  3. Proportional Allocation Assumption: AB 604 analysis assumes voters are distributed within precincts proportionally to registered voters. Actual turnout patterns may differ.

  4. Block Assignment Timing: Census blocks may have changed between 2020 census and 2024 election, introducing minor geographic discrepancies.

  5. No Geographic Context: These metrics don’t account for Communities of Interest, geographic constraints, or Voting Rights Act requirements that may justify certain district shapes.

  6. Statistical Thresholds: There is no universally agreed threshold for “acceptable” levels of these metrics. Courts have used various standards.

Will the adoption of the new district map (AB 604) lead to an increase in partisan advantage relative to 2024?

Answer: Based on our analysis:

  • Seat Distribution: AB 604 would result in 4 additional Democratic seats
  • Mean-Median Score: Changed from -0.0132 to 0.0287 (change: +0.0418)
  • Efficiency Gap: Changed from -0.2306 to -0.1843 (change: +0.0464)

The direction and magnitude of these changes suggest AB 604 may increase partisan advantage.
